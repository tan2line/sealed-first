An AI that has seen your answer isn't checking you. It's agreeing with you, in better prose.

A good meeting chair speaks last, because the first opinion in the room anchors everyone after it. AI works the same way, in both directions.

If the AI answers first, you anchor on it. Researchers call this automation bias: a confident, fluent answer pulls your judgment toward it before you've formed one.

If you share your view first, the AI tends to mirror it back. As obvious mistakes get rarer, the errors that slip through are the agreeable ones.

Sealed First fixes the order. You commit first and keep it hidden. The AI answers without seeing you. Then you compare, on the record.

How it works

Four steps, about two minutes.

  1. Seal

    Write the question, your take, and how sure you are. It gets a fingerprint and can't be edited.

  2. Reveal blind

    The AI answers the question alone. It never sees your take.

  3. Judge

    Agree, partly, or disagree. Then held, updated, or switched, with a reason for any change.

  4. Resolve

    When the result is in, mark who was right. That's what the score is built on.

When you and the AI agree, a red-team step asks for the strongest case against both of you. Shared blind spots hide in agreements.

The score

Your judgment retention score.

A number from 0 to 100 over the last 90 days. It's provisional until five seals are resolved, and it splits by domain so you can see where you hold up and where you defer.

You don't need to seal everything. Pick a weekly target, say three decisions that matter, and keep it.

For teams

Is your human in the loop doing anything?

Most organizations say a person reviews every AI recommendation. Few can show that the person forms their own view first. A Sealed First pilot puts one sealed step in front of the AI's recommendation and turns the result into an evidence report.
